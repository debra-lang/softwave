Research & sources

Last reviewed 22 August 2026

Softwave’s guidance is written from the sources below. The app does not claim to treat tinnitus; each experimental feature shows its own evidence level. Last reviewed 2026-08-22.

Guidelines and organisations

  • NIDCD — Tinnitus (National Institute on Deafness and Other Communication Disorders)
  • American Tinnitus Association — Sound Therapy
  • AAO-HNSF Clinical Practice Guideline: Tinnitus (2014)
  • WHO-ITU standard: Safe listening devices and systems
  • InformedHealth.org — Chronic tinnitus: what helps and what doesn’t

Reviews and trials on sound

  • Sound therapy (masking) in the management of tinnitus in adults — Cochrane Review (2018)
  • Tinnitus retraining therapy: mixing point and total masking are equally effective (JAAA, 2012)
  • A mixed-methods trial of broad band noise and nature sounds for tinnitus therapy (2017)
  • Clinical trial on tonal tinnitus with tailor-made notched music training (BMC Neurology, 2016)
  • Systematic review of sound stimulation to elicit tinnitus residual inhibition (ASHA Evidence Maps)
  • Harvard Health — Sound therapy is one option for tinnitus (2021)

Pitch matching

  • Self-administered tinnitus pitch matching versus a conventional audiometric procedure (2017)
  • Evaluation of iPod-based automated tinnitus pitch matching (2015)

Attention, relaxation and sleep

  • Mindfulness-based cognitive therapy for chronic tinnitus: RCT (2017)
  • Auditory and visual inputs for relaxation during VR stimulation (2022)
  • Sleep Foundation — How to sleep with tinnitus

How to read the evidence labels in Experiments

  • Well-studied principle — multiple trials or guideline support for the underlying idea (not necessarily for the app feature).
  • Promising research — at least one controlled trial, modest or short-lived effects.
  • Experimental — research is limited — pilots, mechanism papers, or evidence only from a neighbouring field.
  • Experimental — evidence is mixed or limited — conflicting or sparse results.
