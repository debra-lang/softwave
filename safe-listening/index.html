Safe listening

Last reviewed 22 August 2026

  • Use the lowest level that helps. Sound should partly cover the tinnitus, not overpower it. Most people settle just below or around their tinnitus.
  • Mind headphone time. The WHO-ITU safe-listening standard suggests personal audio around 80 dB or less for no more than 40 hours a week (75 dB for children). Keep device volume around 60% or less.
  • Take breaks. Prolonged loud listening can harm hearing and may make tinnitus more noticeable.
  • Start quiet, adjust slowly. Softwave starts every sound low and fades it in.
  • Speakers are fine — often better for all-night use.
  • Sound sensitivity (hyperacusis): stay at the quietest end; the Near-Silence tool exists for this. Seek a clinician’s plan if everyday sounds hurt.

How the app protects you

35% default level, never restored above 60%; fade-ins and fade-outs on everything; a limiter on the mix; a warning above 75%; tones capped lower than noise; nothing raises the volume by itself.

Important: Softwave is designed for sound masking, relaxation and tinnitus management support. It does not diagnose, treat or cure tinnitus and is not a substitute for professional medical care. If your tinnitus is new, in one ear only, pulsing with your heartbeat, or comes with hearing loss or dizziness, see a doctor or audiologist.
