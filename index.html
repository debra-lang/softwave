Listen well

A few simple principles from tinnitus and hearing-health organisations.

Safe listening

  • Use the lowest level that helps. Sound therapy works best as a gentle background — partly covering the tinnitus, not overpowering it. Most people find a level just below or around their tinnitus most comfortable.
  • Mind headphone time. The WHO safe-listening standard suggests keeping personal audio around 80 dB or below for no more than 40 hours a week. A good habit: keep your device volume around 60% or less.
  • Take breaks. Prolonged high-volume headphone use can harm hearing and may make tinnitus more noticeable.
  • Start quiet, adjust slowly. Softwave always starts sounds at a soft level and fades them in; raise volume gradually.
  • Speakers are fine. A speaker or a pillow speaker at night can be just as useful as headphones.

When to talk to a professional

Sound tools can help day to day, but please see a doctor, audiologist or ENT (ear, nose and throat) specialist if your tinnitus:

  • is new, or changed suddenly
  • is persistent (around six months or more) or is bothering you a lot
  • is only in one ear
  • comes with hearing difficulty, fullness in the ear, or dizziness
  • pulses with your heartbeat
  • started after an injury, or is affecting sleep, mood or concentration

A hearing evaluation is the usual first step. Evidence-based options such as hearing aids, sound therapy and cognitive behavioural therapy are available, and professionals can help you choose.

How sound can help

Tinnitus often feels loudest in silence. Adding a pleasant, steady sound can make it less noticeable, give your attention something else to rest on, and help with relaxation and sleep. Over time many people find the tinnitus fades further into the background.

There is no single best sound. Broadband noise (white, pink, brown) and natural sounds are the most widely used; what matters is what feels comfortable to you.

Privacy

Softwave works without an account. Your favourites, presets and settings are stored only on this device. Nothing about your hearing is collected or sent anywhere, and the optional "Find My Sound" result is saved only if you choose to.

Important

This app is designed for sound masking, relaxation, and tinnitus management support. It does not diagnose, treat, or cure tinnitus and is not a substitute for professional medical care.

Sources: NIDCD (National Institute on Deafness and Other Communication Disorders), American Tinnitus Association, AAO-HNS Clinical Practice Guideline: Tinnitus, WHO-ITU safe-listening standard.
